## [OpenAI Astra Model Breaks New Ground on Ten Long-Standing Math Problems](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

OpenAI announced that an internal version of its next-generation Astra model achieved new results on ten long-standing open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science, including high-dimensional sphere packing, existence of non-sofic groups, a counterexample to Connes' rigidity conjecture, arithmetic circuit lower bounds, quantum parallel repetition, hardness of the nearest vector problem, and multicolor Ramsey numbers. The arguments were compiled into papers by human–AI collaboration and formally verified in the Lean proof assistant, with token costs around $2,000. If independently verified, these results would mark a paradigm shift: an AI system autonomously producing original mathematical proofs that withstand formal verification. This could dramatically accelerate research in mathematics and theoretical computer science and change how mathematicians collaborate with AI. OpenAI openly states that the mathematical arguments themselves were generated by AI, while humans curated and formalized them, and says authorship should reflect the source of the results. The company emphasizes the need for broad access during this transition and invites the mathematical community to scrutinize the results, with external peer review still pending.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 1, 07:59

**Background**: Lean is an open-source proof assistant and functional programming language based on the Calculus of Inductive Constructions, widely used to formally verify mathematical theorems. Sofic groups, introduced by Gromov, are a class of groups that can be approximated by finite symmetric groups, and proving the existence of a non-sofic group has been a major open problem. Connes' rigidity conjecture, posed in 1980, asks whether certain group factors determine the underlying group; a counterexample would refute that conjecture. Formal verification in Lean gives high confidence in logical correctness, but mathematical acceptance and peer review are separate steps.

## [Stateless MCP 2.0 Reignites Interest in Model Context Protocol](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/31/stateless-mcp/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Anthropic rolled out MCP 2.0 (the 2026-07-28 Model Context Protocol specification), making the protocol stateless. Simon Willison built three new implementations this week, including mcp-explorer and datasette-mcp, citing renewed interest. This is the most significant change to MCP since its launch, greatly simplifying client and server implementation and improving scalability. It could steer the agent ecosystem back toward auditable, controllable MCP tools rather than relying on unrestricted terminal access. The new stateless design uses a single HTTP request, with protocol details carried in headers such as MCP-Protocol-Version and Mcp-Method, eliminating the initialization step and session IDs. This removes server-side session state, making applications easier to scale, though it may increase repetitive data in requests.

rss · Simon Willison · Jul 31, 23:13

**Background**: The Model Context Protocol (MCP) is an open standard introduced by Anthropic in November 2024 for connecting AI assistants to external tools and data sources. The previous stateful version required two HTTP requests: one to initialize a session and obtain a session ID, and another to call a tool. In contrast, a stateless protocol treats each request independently, improving visibility, reliability, and scalability. Earlier in 2025, MCP's popularity was overshadowed by Anthropic's Skills, but the new stateless spec has renewed interest.

## [Study: Go AI Networks Learn Orientation-Invariant Representations](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1vcrki2/how_symmetric_are_the_insides_of_a_go_network_r/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

KataGo's author published an interpretability study examining how superhuman Go neural networks handle board symmetry. Despite relying only on stochastic 8-fold data augmentation during training, the networks learned largely orientation-independent internal representations, and one finding was unexpected. This study offers rare insight into how neural networks spontaneously learn known symmetries from data, which has implications for interpretability, data augmentation, and architecture design. It also provides a deep, accessible look into the internals of a top open-source Go AI. The study and its write-up were largely AI-driven, with detailed human direction and feedback, and the article was deliberately written to be accessible to non-ML readers. Code and the full write-up are linked from the Reddit post, and the author notes that one result was unexpected.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/icosaplex · Aug 1, 16:18

**Background**: Go is a board game whose rules are completely symmetric under rotation and reflection. KataGo, an open-source superhuman Go program developed by David Wu, does not enforce this symmetry architecturally; instead, it uses stochastic 8-fold data augmentation that randomly rotates or reflects each training batch. This study asks whether the network's internal representations nevertheless become orientation-independent on their own.

## [ChangXin Memory unveils 8000Mbps DDR5 and LPDDR5X at IC China](https://t.me/zaihuapd/42925) ⭐️ 8.0/10

At the 22nd China International Semiconductor Exposition (IC China), ChangXin Memory showcased its latest DDR5 and LPDDR5X product lines for the first time. The new DDR5 modules reach up to 8000Mbps, a 25% speed improvement over mainstream 6400Mbps products. This launch marks ChangXin Memory's entry into the international top-tier memory performance group, directly competing with established players like Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron. The high-speed DDR5 and high-capacity LPDDR5X solutions are particularly relevant for data centers and mobile devices, strengthening China's domestic semiconductor memory ecosystem. The DDR5 family also includes a maximum 24Gb capacity die, addressing rapid data center capacity expansion needs. The LPDDR5X line targets the mobile market with a peak speed of 10667Mbps, the largest die capacity of 16Gb, and packaged solutions ranging from 12GB to 32GB.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 1, 15:30

**Background**: DDR5 SDRAM is the fifth generation of double data rate synchronous dynamic random-access memory, succeeding DDR4. It doubles bandwidth, reduces power consumption, and incorporates on-die error correction, making it widely used in PCs and servers. LPDDR5X is a low-power variant of DRAM optimized for mobile devices, offering high bandwidth while minimizing battery drain. These memory technologies are fundamental to computing performance, especially in data centers and AI workloads.
